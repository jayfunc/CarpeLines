CarpeLines

The mark for word-by-word lyrics: three lyric lines and one time cursor. The cursor sits at 59% of the line width, marking the character being sung right now.

Material 3 Expressive Android adaptive icon 2026-11
WORDMARK
CarpeLines

The wordmark and the mark are used separately. The mark carries all of the recognition; the wordmark is a single-weight typographic treatment — no colour added, no outline, no two-tone split.

01

Concept

CarpeLines promises that, whatever is playing on the phone, the lyrics keep pace word by word. The mark does one thing: it draws that promise into a shape you understand at a glance. Three capsules are lyric lines; one perfect circle is the time cursor.

Lines

Three lines, a broken right edge

Three equal horizontal lines would be read as a menu icon. So the lines are 50 / 59 / 30 — an uneven right edge is what makes them look like a line being read.

Carpe

The cursor sits at 59%

The circle is the playback position and also "now" — what Carpe means. It is 1.75× the line height and overshoots 3 above and below: the only element in the whole mark that leaves the grid.

Shape contrast

Circle against capsule, no colour shift

M3E treats shape as an expressive lever alongside colour. The cursor earns its focus through shape difference, so the whole mark needs only two ink levels.

Why not the old note + lines: a note says "plays music", but this app plays no local audio — it only follows the system media session and shows the lyrics. The mark should point at the thing this app alone does: aligning lyrics with time.

02

Construction

Every dimension is based on the 108×108 adaptive-icon canvas (the Android spec) and quantised to multiples of 4. But launchers only keep the middle 72 × 72, so the glyph is output at 2/3 of the canvas — otherwise the edges get cropped to 3–4dp on screen and the whitespace no longer matches the design.

04

Colour

Paper white as the base, vermilion and warm grey as the strokes, deep ink reserved for the cursor. The moment the base drifts yellow and the strokes drift brown, the whole surface slides towards "caramel on old paper" — so the base is pushed almost white and colour appears only on the line being sung.

The mark does not take part in dynamic colour. At runtime the app derives 48 semantic roles from the album artwork, but brand assets must stay constant — otherwise the same mark looks different on every phone. The values here are therefore hard-coded; they mirror primary (sung), the dark tone of primary (unsung) and onSurface (cursor / wordmark), but they are brand constants and never read from ColorScheme.

05

Variants

Two levels: light background (the only colour version, the default) and a flat silhouette. The mono version serves Android 13+ themed icons, the notification bar and single-colour print.

Colour  paper-white base + vermilion strokes, the default and only colour version  ·  Mono  flat silhouette, themed icons / notification icon

06

Lockups

The mark height and the wordmark cap height hold an 84 : 30 ratio. The wordmark appears once, with at least one mark-width of whitespace to its right.

Left: vertical lockup, for splash screens and store art. Right: bare mark (no container), for tight spaces or surfaces that already carry the brand colour.

07

Motion

Motion explains one thing only: where the song has reached. The cursor travels with the playback position, and the highlight advances from the cursor's left edge and never crosses it.

SegmentTokenSpec

Gradient driftsplash / store art only8s loop; still when idle

When the system's remove-animations setting is on, only the resting frame remains (cursor at 59%). The resting frame has to be a complete, credible mark on its own — which is why animation is not part of the mark, only an explanation of it.

08

Usage

DO

This is the only colour icon; dark UI reuses it instead of getting a dark variant.

DO

Use the silhouette where colour is unavailable and let the system tint it.

DON'T

No drop shadows. Hierarchy comes from tone, not shadow.

DON'T

Do not stretch or rotate. The proportions are part of the mark.

DON'T

Do not shift the hue. The UI already takes colour from the artwork; the mark must stay constant.

DON'T

No translucency. The unsung lines sink into the background with it.

09

M3E compliance check

Checked point by point against the six expressive levers of M3 Expressive and the conventions already set out in the project's M3E-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md.

LeverWhat this mark does
ColourPaper-white base + vermilion / warm grey (#D9480F / #8A7663); deep ink only for cursor and wordmark; the container carries a single same-hue gradient
ShapeCapsule (lines) against perfect circle (cursor). Shape difference carries the whole hierarchy; colour only assists
ScaleLine height 8 / cursor Ø14 = 1.75× (output 5.3 / 9.3); the cursor is the only element that leaves the grid
Containerr30 rounded-square container + tonal gradient (matching "express depth with tone, not shadow")
MotionExplains state only (where the song is), never produces a decorative loop
TypeWordmark at a single weight of 700, tracking −0.02em; no colour, no bold line breaks
RestraintExactly one strong expressive point in the whole mark (the cursor); sung and unsung differ by one level of lightness, so colour never competes with the cursor
AccessibilitySung 3.5–4.0:1, unsung 3.5–4.0:1, cursor / wordmark 14.3–16.3:1; the mono version still reads at 16px

One deliberate deviation: the design system forbids bringing content colours below 38% alpha. The "unsung" stroke here does not use alpha but the solid #8A7663 — 38% deep ink on a paper-white base measures below the 3:1 floor for graphics, while the solid colour holds above 3.5:1 at both ends of the gradient.
